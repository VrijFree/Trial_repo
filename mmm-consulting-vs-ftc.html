3. Is MMM a regular process at large and small clients?

Not universal. Reported adoption rises steeply with marketing spend; these figures are from a vendor-published, US-centric study (presenc.ai), so treat them as direction only:

  • B2B mid-market and enterprise: 31% adoption versus 14% for companies under $10M ARR.
  • One practitioner post advises small e-commerce companies to skip MMM and focus on simpler measurement sourced, Japan e-commerce, one opinion.
  • Implication for you: large NL/DE advertisers are the natural MMM buyers; SMBs mostly buy lighter products (platform dashboards, simple regressions, open-source starter builds). judgement

4. How long are outputs used, and when is a refresh needed?

  • Cadence: quarterly or semi-annual is the common default; monthly suits e-commerce and retail with fast promo cycles and good data; modern automated pipelines can refresh weekly or monthly sourced.
  • Shelf life: models degrade as the market changes, so refreshes add data, recalibrate and compare coefficients with the previous version; large shifts in coefficients signal real market change to investigate sourced.
  • Refresh triggers judgement: new or paused channels, big budget reallocation, pricing or promo strategy change, acquisition or new market, a market shock, new data (e.g. a lift test result), the annual planning cycle. A Monks article lists "5 factors" for when to refresh; I could not read it, so check it yourself: Monks.
  • Usual rhythm judgement: one large annual build tied to budget planning, plus quarterly refreshes; clients without automated data often stay at semi-annual or annual.

5. Who holds the data, and what goes wrong

Who typically has access judgement

Several teams each hold a piece: media agency (spend, plans), marketing/performance team (platform exports), finance (revenue, margin), BI/CRM team (sales, customers), commercial/trade team (price, promo), market research (brand tracking). The consultant usually gets data through a single client lead who has to collect from all of them.

Sourced challenges

Data sits in separate platforms and consolidation is slow and error-prone; campaigns across 15–30 platforms with inconsistent schemas; about 70% of effort goes to data prep; agency media inputs are hard to verify; long collection timelines; privacy rules reduce granularity; regional data gives more accurate models than national data. sourced German guides add: data quality decides everything, automate pipelines, and budget for integration, possibly a data integration engineer, especially for the Mittelstand.

Market-specific points judgement

NL: small country, so mostly national models; agency-held media data is common. Belgium: two language markets, so TV and media data may need splitting by region. Germany: 16 federal states allow regional modelling and larger sample sizes, but also a more fragmented data landscape; German-language documentation expected. All: GDPR consent limits web data; euro as a common currency helps.
